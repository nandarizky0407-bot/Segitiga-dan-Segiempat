Angles on a straight line

Remember: angles on a straight line always add up to 180°. Two angles that form a straight line are called supplementary angles (berpelurus).

65° + x = 180°
x = 180° − a° → subtract the known angle from 180°.

Proof lab

Why do the angles always make 180°?

1 · Draw any triangle 2 · Tear off its 3 corners 3 · Line them up — they fit a straight line!
?°+?°+?°=?°

A straight line measures 180°. The three torn corners sit side by side and fill the line perfectly — no gaps, no overlaps. That is why every triangle's angles total 180°!

Press “Try another triangle” — big, small, skinny or wide… the result is always 180°.
